Equipment Proficiencies
Weapons. Anyone can wield a weapon, but
proficiency allows you to add your
Proficiency Bonus to attack rolls made with
it. Weapon proficiency does not add extra
damage unless a rule says otherwise.
Tools. Tool proficiency allows you to add
your Proficiency Bonus to an ability check
made with that tool. If both a relevant skill
and tool proficiency apply, add your
Proficiency Bonus once and make the check
with Advantage.


Expertise
Expertise represents exceptional mastery of
a proficiency. When you make an ability
check using a skill or tool in which you
have Expertise, double your Proficiency
Bonus for that check.
You cannot benefit from Expertise more
than once on the same check. You still add
your Proficiency Bonus only once before
doubling it.

Conditions
A condition is a temporary state that
changes what a creature can do. A condition
lasts until its stated duration ends or another
rule removes it. Conditions do not stack
with themselves; a creature either has a
condition or does not. Exhaustion is the
exception. See all conditions in the
Conditions table.

Duration. A condition lasts until its source
says it ends.
Repeated Conditions. Multiple instances
don’t intensify the condition, but their
durations are tracked separately. Exhaustion
is the exception.
Nested Conditions. If a condition gives you
another condition, apply both rows.

                                       Chapter 4
                              Backgrounds
Character Backgrounds
A character’s background is the occupation
that was most formative for the character
before becoming a Hunter. A background
gives your character Proficiency with two
skills, and gives some more points towards
a selection of Ability Scores. A Background
may also give your character a Feat,
Proficiency with a tool, or various starting
Equipment. You can choose whatever
background you want.

PARTS OF A BACKGROUND
A background includes the following parts.
Ability Scores. A background lists three of
your character's ability scores. Increase one
by 2 and another one by 1, or increase all
three by 1. None of these increases can raise
a score above 20.
Feat. A background may give your
character a specified Origin feat (described
in chapter 5).
Skill Proficiencies. A background gives
your character proficiency in two specified
skills.
Tool Proficiency. Each background may
give a character proficiency with one tool.
Tools are detailed in chapter 6.
Equipment. Each background can offer
additional equipment.

Improvised Weapons
If you use an object—such as a table leg,
frying pan, or bottle—as a makeshift
weapon, see the rules below. Also see those
rules if you wield a weapon in an unusual
way, such as using a Ranged weapon to
make a melee attack.

An improvised weapon is an object that was
not designed to be used as a weapon, or a
normal weapon used in an unintended
manner.

A melee improvised weapon deals 1d4
Bludgeoning, Piercing, or Slashing damage,
as determined by the GM, and uses Strength
for its attack and damage rolls.

A thrown improvised weapon has a normal
range of 20 feet and a long range of 60 feet.
It deals 1d4 damage of a type determined by
the GM and uses Dexterity for its attack and
damage rolls.
